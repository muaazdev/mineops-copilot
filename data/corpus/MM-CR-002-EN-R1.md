# Crusher Main Bearing Lubrication and Temperature Monitoring

## 1. Purpose

Describes lubrication of the crusher main (eccentric shaft) bearings and the response to high bearing temperature alarms, which are the leading indicator of bearing failure.

## 2. Scope

Jaw and cone crushers fitted with circulating oil lubrication and bearing RTD sensors.

## 3. Responsibilities

The maintenance superintendent owns this manual and keeps it aligned with the manufacturer's recommendations. Maintenance planners schedule the tasks in the maintenance system. Technicians perform the tasks, record measurements on the work order and report any condition outside the stated limits to their supervisor.

## 4. Personal Protective Equipment

- Safety helmet
- Safety glasses
- Nitrile gloves
- Hearing protection

## 5. Procedure

1. Check the lube oil tank level, oil temperature and filter differential pressure at the start of every shift.
2. The main bearing temperature alarm is set at 85 °C and the automatic trip at 95 °C; never bypass the trip.
3. On an 85 °C alarm, reduce feed, check oil flow and cooler operation, and notify maintenance.
4. After any trip on high bearing temperature, take an oil sample and inspect the magnetic plug for metal before restart.
5. Two or more high-temperature trips on the same bearing within 30 days require a bearing inspection work order (priority high).
6. Change the lube oil filter at a differential pressure of 1.5 bar or every 500 hours.
7. Record bearing temperatures hourly on the crusher log during the first 24 hours after any bearing work.

## 6. Hazards and Controls

- Bearing seizure and shaft damage — respond to alarms, never bypass the trip.
- Hot oil — allow the system to cool before opening the lube circuit.
- Rotating parts — isolate per LOTO-003 before any inspection.

## 7. Records and Training

Completed checklists, permits and work orders are retained in the site document system for at least five years. Workers complete training on this document before first use and refresher training every two years, or sooner when the document is revised.
