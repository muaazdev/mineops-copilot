# Hazardous Substance Spill Response

## 1. Purpose

Describes the response to spills of diesel, hydraulic oil, process reagents and explosives precursors.

## 2. Scope

All sites, including fuel bays, workshops, the processing plant and the explosives magazine.

## 3. Responsibilities

The emergency response coordinator maintains this plan and runs drills at least every six months. The control room coordinates the response and external services. Every person on site knows the alarm signals, the muster points and their role in an emergency.

## 4. Personal Protective Equipment

- Chemical-resistant gloves
- Safety glasses or face shield
- Chemical-resistant apron
- Respirator as per the safety data sheet

## 5. Procedure

1. Stop the source of the spill if it is safe to do so (close the valve, stop the pump, upright the container).
2. Isolate the area and keep ignition sources at least 15 m away from flammable spills.
3. Check the safety data sheet (SDS) for the substance before choosing PPE and the clean-up method.
4. Contain the spill with spill kit booms or earth bunds to stop it reaching drains or water courses.
5. Spills larger than 200 litres, or any spill of ammonium nitrate, must be reported to the environmental officer within 1 hour.
6. Collect contaminated soil and absorbent into labelled hazardous waste bins.

## 6. Hazards and Controls

- Fire from flammable vapours — ignition control within 15 m.
- Skin and eye exposure — SDS-based PPE.
- Environmental contamination — containment and reporting.

## 7. Records and Training

Completed checklists, permits and work orders are retained in the site document system for at least five years. Workers complete training on this document before first use and refresher training every two years, or sooner when the document is revised.
