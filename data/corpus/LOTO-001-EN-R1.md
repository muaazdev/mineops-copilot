# Lockout/Tagout — General Isolation Procedure

## 1. Purpose

Defines how hazardous energy is isolated, locked, tagged and verified before any person works on plant or mobile equipment at Falcon Ridge Mining sites.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all employees and contractors performing maintenance, cleaning, inspection or unjamming on equipment with electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic or gravitational energy.

## 3. Responsibilities

The site manager is accountable for implementing this document. Supervisors ensure that workers are trained and competent before they perform the tasks described, and they verify compliance during field inspections. Every worker follows this document, stops work when conditions are unsafe, and reports hazards and incidents to their supervisor without delay.

## 4. Personal Protective Equipment

- Safety helmet
- Safety glasses
- Arc-rated gloves when operating isolators
- Steel-capped boots

## 5. Procedure

1. Notify the area supervisor of the planned isolation.
2. Identify the energy sources for the equipment.
3. Stop the equipment using the normal stop control.
4. Open the isolator and apply a danger lock and tag.
5. Up to three workers may share a single danger lock on the isolation point, provided all names are written on the tag.
6. Release stored energy where practical.
7. Verify zero energy by attempting to start the equipment.
8. On completion, the lock holder removes the lock after confirming all persons are clear.

## 6. Hazards and Controls

- Unexpected start-up from a missed energy source — controlled by the isolation sheet and test-before-touch.
- Stored hydraulic or gravitational energy — controlled by bleeding and mechanical blocking.
- Removal of another person's lock — prohibited; lock removal by force requires the site manager's approval.

## 7. Records and Training

Completed checklists, permits and work orders are retained in the site document system for at least five years. Workers complete training on this document before first use and refresher training every two years, or sooner when the document is revised.
