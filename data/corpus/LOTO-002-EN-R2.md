# Conveyor Isolation and Belt Restraint

## 1. Purpose

Describes the isolation of belt conveyors before work on pulleys, idlers, scrapers, chutes or the belt itself.

## 2. Scope

All belt conveyors including overland, plant feed and stacker conveyors.

## 3. Responsibilities

The site manager is accountable for implementing this document. Supervisors ensure that workers are trained and competent before they perform the tasks described, and they verify compliance during field inspections. Every worker follows this document, stops work when conditions are unsafe, and reports hazards and incidents to their supervisor without delay.

## 4. Personal Protective Equipment

- Safety helmet
- Safety glasses
- Hearing protection
- Cut-resistant gloves
- High-visibility vest

## 5. Procedure

1. Run the conveyor empty before stopping where the process allows.
2. Isolate the drive motor at the motor control centre (MCC) and apply personal locks per LOTO-001.
3. Isolate the take-up winch or release gravity take-up tension using the take-up lock pin.
4. On conveyors inclined more than 8 degrees, fit the mechanical belt clamp below the work area to prevent belt rollback.
5. Test the pull-wire emergency stop and the local stop station; they must not be used as isolation points.
6. Verify zero energy by attempting a start from the local station.
7. Remove the belt clamp only after all tools and persons are clear.

## 6. Hazards and Controls

- Belt rollback on inclined sections — controlled by the mechanical belt clamp.
- Stored tension in the take-up — controlled by pinning or lowering the counterweight.
- Nip points at pulleys and idlers — guards remain fitted until isolation is verified.

## 7. Records and Training

Completed checklists, permits and work orders are retained in the site document system for at least five years. Workers complete training on this document before first use and refresher training every two years, or sooner when the document is revised.
