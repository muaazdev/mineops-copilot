# Heat Stress Management

## 1. Purpose

Protects workers from heat illness during the hot season, using the wet bulb globe temperature (WBGT) index.

## 2. Scope

All outdoor and plant work at all sites, May to October in particular.

## 3. Responsibilities

The site manager is accountable for implementing this document. Supervisors ensure that workers are trained and competent before they perform the tasks described, and they verify compliance during field inspections. Every worker follows this document, stops work when conditions are unsafe, and reports hazards and incidents to their supervisor without delay.

## 4. Personal Protective Equipment

- Wide-brim helmet cover
- Light-coloured long-sleeve clothing
- Cooling vest where issued

## 5. Procedure

1. The site control room publishes the WBGT reading every hour on the radio and on the site screens.
2. WBGT 28 to 30 °C — drink 250 ml of water every 15 minutes and work in shaded areas where possible.
3. WBGT 30 to 32 °C — 45 minutes work and 15 minutes rest in a cooled shelter.
4. WBGT 32 to 34 °C — 30 minutes work and 30 minutes rest; no lone working.
5. WBGT above 34 °C — stop all non-essential outdoor work.
6. Outdoor work between 12:00 and 15:00 from 15 June to 15 September requires a heat stress permit.
7. Report symptoms of heat exhaustion (dizziness, headache, cramps) immediately; move the person to shade and cool them.

## 6. Hazards and Controls

- Heat exhaustion and heat stroke — WBGT-based work-rest cycles and hydration.
- Dehydration — water stations every 200 m in the pit.
- Hot surfaces on equipment — gloves and shade.

## 7. Records and Training

Completed checklists, permits and work orders are retained in the site document system for at least five years. Workers complete training on this document before first use and refresher training every two years, or sooner when the document is revised.
